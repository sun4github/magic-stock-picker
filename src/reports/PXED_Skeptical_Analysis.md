# Research Report: Phoenix Education Partners, Inc. (NYSE: PXED)

---

## Basic Company Info Questions

### 1. What products or services does the company actually sell?
Phoenix Education Partners, Inc. (parent company of the University of Phoenix) sells online undergraduate and graduate degree programs—primarily Bachelor’s, Master’s, Associate’s, and Doctoral degrees—along with non-degree professional certificates and workforce upskilling courses.

### 2. Who are its primary customers?
Its primary customers are adult working learners in the United States. Over 95% of new students are over age 22, with an average age of 38. Additionally, 75% are currently employed, 71% are female, 53% are first-generation college students, and 32% enroll through corporate B2B employer partnerships.

### 3. How does the company make money?
The company derives nearly all its revenue from student tuition and fee payments, which are frequently funded through Title IV federal financial aid programs (Pell Grants and federal student loans), employer tuition assistance programs, or direct out-of-pocket payments.

### 4. Who are its two closest publicly traded competitors that compete most directly in the company’s largest revenue source, and what makes this company different from them? Explain why you chose those two.
The two closest publicly traded competitors are **Strategic Education, Inc. (STRA)** (owner of Capella University and Strayer University) and **Universal Technical Institute, Inc. (UTI)** (and formerly American Public Education, Inc. APEI). 
* **Strategic Education (STRA)** was chosen because it is the most direct online higher education peer focused on working adults and degree programs.
* **Universal Technical Institute (UTI)** was chosen as a leading publicly traded proprietary post-secondary education provider operating in career-focused education.
* **Key Difference:** Phoenix Education Partners is distinguished by its sheer historical scale (over 1.1 million alumni and 81,900 average annual degreed enrollment in FY2025) and its 100% online/asynchronous delivery format (having closed virtually all physical campuses except its Phoenix headquarters). However, unlike STRA, PXED is heavily controlled by private equity firm Apollo Global Management following its October 2025 IPO.

### 5. Write one sentence I could use to explain this business to a friend with no investing experience.
"Phoenix Education Partners runs the University of Phoenix, making money by selling online college degrees and career training courses primarily to working adults."

### 6. What is one common misunderstanding or oversimplification that could lead an investor to analyze this company incorrectly?
A common misunderstanding is assuming that because the University of Phoenix is an online brand, its customer acquisition costs (CAC) are fixed or declining. In reality, attracting adult students requires massive, continuous advertising expenditures; when search algorithms change or prospective leads decline, marketing efficiency drops rapidly, hurting profits even if course delivery costs are low.

### 7. Using the most recent annual report, what are the main business segments of the company, which generates the most revenue, and which generates the most profit? If those are different segments, explain why.
Based on disclosures in the FY2025 Annual Report (Form 10-K), the company operates primarily as a single reportable educational segment focused on higher education offerings:
* **Degree-Granting Programs:** Generated approximately **97%** of net revenue in FY2025.
* **Non-Degree Certificate & Skill Offerings:** Generated the remaining **3%** of net revenue.
Because the business is managed as a single unified operation, separate segment profit breakdowns are not reported; degree-granting programs generate virtually all revenue and profit.

## Business Model Questions

### 1. What is the business model of the company?
PXED operates a technology-enabled, distance-education platform model. It acquires adult students via digital marketing and employer relationships, delivers asynchronous online coursework through proprietary tech stacks, and collects tuition fees per credit hour or term.

### 2. Is revenue recurring, one-time, or repeat but non-contractual?
Revenue is **repeat but non-contractual**. Students enroll on a course-by-course or term-by-term basis. While students who remain in a multi-year degree program provide a pseudo-recurring revenue stream, students can withdraw at any time, requiring constant top-of-funnel lead generation to replace graduating or dropping students.

### 3. Where does pricing power come from?
Pricing power is relatively **weak**. It depends on perceived career utility and tuition affordability relative to traditional non-profit universities and community colleges. Increasing tuition too aggressively risks losing price-sensitive adult learners or failing Title IV federal gainful employment and affordability benchmarks.

### 4. What would happen to revenue if their best-selling product was made obsolete by a competitor tomorrow?
The company’s top degree disciplines (such as Business and Information Technology) generate a significant portion of enrollments. If a competitor offered a cheaper, faster, or superior alternative that rendered its core business degrees uncompetitive, revenue would contract rapidly due to high student churn and falling enrollment conversion.

### 5. Based on disclosures in the most recent annual report, are any customers responsible for more than 10% of revenue?
**Not disclosed** as having any single customer exceeding 10% of revenue. The student base is highly fragmented across tens of thousands of individual adult learners, though Title IV federal financial aid program funding collectively accounts for the vast majority of cash inflows.

### 6. Based on the most recent annual report, what percentage of revenue comes from the top product or business segment? How has that percentage changed over the last three years?
Degree-granting programs generated approximately **97%** of net revenue in FY2025. This percentage has remained largely stable (around 97%–98%) over the past three fiscal years, as non-degree certificates remain a small, albeit growing, secondary priority (~3%).

### 7. Which segment generates the most profit, and is that different from the one generating the most revenue?
Degree-granting programs generate both the vast majority of revenue (~97%) and the vast majority of profit. There is no structural difference in reporting segments.

## Risk Analysis

### 1. Three Biggest Specific Risks (3–5 Year Horizon)

1. **AI Search Disruption & Rising Customer Acquisition Costs (CAC):**
   * *Type:* Company-Specific / Industry-Wide | *Timing:* Gradual to Acceleration.
   * *Detail:* Prospective students increasingly use AI conversational assistants rather than traditional Google search engine links. This reduces organic search visibility for the University of Phoenix, forcing higher paid ad spend that yields lower conversion rates.
2. **Title IV Regulatory & Legislative Changes (OBBB Act Impact):**
   * *Type:* Macro / Industry-Wide | *Timing:* Sudden (Effective July 2026).
   * *Detail:* Amendments under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBB) eliminate federal Grad PLUS loans and establish strict lifetime Title IV borrowing caps ($257,500), while conditioning program eligibility on graduate median earnings benchmarks.
3. **Private Equity Governance & Selling Pressure:**
   * *Type:* Company-Specific | *Timing:* Sudden.
   * *Detail:* Apollo Global Management retains controlling interest post-IPO. Potential secondary share dump or decisions favoring sponsor fees over minority shareholder returns pose significant overhang.

## Final Verdict

Before deciding, we weigh the two sides:
* **BULL Case:** Surfaced at **#6** on the Magic Formula screen with an extraordinary Return on Capital of **118.35%**, an Earnings Yield of **23.0%**, a low P/E (~10-13x), and a debt-free net cash balance sheet ($103M+ working capital).
* **BEAR Case:** Top-line growth stalled in Q3 FY2026; management cut FY2026 revenue guidance; organic student lead generation is being disrupted by AI search tools; CAC is rising; and PE sponsor Apollo holds controlling power.

Verdict: Watch

**Justification:** 
PXED presents a classic tension between statistical cheapness and fundamental operational deterioration. On paper, a #6 Magic Formula ranking with a 118% Return on Capital and a 23% Earnings Yield suggests an absolute bargain. However, the Q3 FY2026 earnings miss and revenue guidance cut explicitly exposed structural vulnerabilities in PXED’s core digital marketing funnel due to AI search disruption and rising customer acquisition costs. Until management demonstrates that its employer B2B partnerships can stabilize student enrollment conversion without eroding margins through steep tuition discounts, initiating a new position carries falling-knife risk. The risk-reward is balanced enough to monitor on a watchlist rather than buying immediately or avoiding entirely.
