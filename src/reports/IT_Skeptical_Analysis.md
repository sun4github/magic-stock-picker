# Research Report: Gartner, Inc. (NYSE: IT)

---

## Basic Company Info Questions

### 1. What products or services does the company actually sell?
Gartner sells three main offerings:
* **Business and Technology Insights ("Insights"):** Subscription-based access to research reports, vendor evaluation tools (such as its flagship *Magic Quadrants*), proprietary data benchmarks, and direct advisor conversations with over 2,400 global experts.
* **Conferences:** Large in-person and virtual industry events (such as the *Gartner IT Symposium/Xpo* series) where technology and business executives network and learn about industry trends.
* **Consulting:** Direct custom advisory services that assist Chief Information Officers (CIOs) and senior executives with enterprise technology strategy, cost optimization, and digital transformation projects.

### 2. Who are its primary customers?
Its primary customers are Chief Information Officers (CIOs), Chief Technology Officers (CTOs), and enterprise leaders across key functional areas—including Finance, Human Resources, Supply Chain, and Marketing—in more than 13,000 organizations spanning approximately 90 countries.

### 3. How does the company make money?
Gartner earns money through three revenue streams:
* High-margin, multi-year, and annual **subscription software/content fees** paid upfront for its Insights research platform.
* **Ticket sales and corporate sponsorship fees** for its global industry conferences.
* **Custom consulting fees** billed on a project or retainer basis.

### 4. Who are its two closest publicly traded competitors that compete most directly in the company’s largest revenue source, and what makes this company different from them? Explain why you chose those two.
The two closest publicly traded competitors in enterprise IT advisory and services are **CGI Inc. (GIB)** and **Leidos Holdings, Inc. (LDOS)**.
* **Why chosen:** Both CGI and Leidos directly compete for the attention and budgets of enterprise CIOs, offering strategic technology guidance, market evaluation, and IT implementation support.
* **What makes Gartner different:** Gartner operates primarily as a subscription-based research publisher with high gross margins (~68%), selling standardized intellectual property and analyst calls rather than relying heavily on labor-intensive, billable-hour IT consulting and system integration contracts.

### 5. Write one sentence I could use to explain this business to a friend with no investing experience.
Gartner is like a subscription-based "Consumer Reports" for big corporations, helping business leaders decide which expensive software, hardware, and tech strategies they should buy or avoid.

### 6. What is one common misunderstanding or oversimplification that could lead an investor to analyze this company incorrectly?
Investors often oversimplify Gartner as a traditional consulting firm that relies on selling human billable hours. In reality, over 80% of its revenue comes from subscription content ("Insights") that scales digitally with near-zero incremental distribution cost, giving it much higher profit potential than traditional labor-heavy consulting firms.

### 7. Using the most recent annual report, what are the main business segments of the company, which generates the most revenue, and which generates the most profit? If those are different segments, explain why.
* **Business Segments:** Business and Technology Insights ("Insights"), Conferences, and Consulting.
* **Highest Revenue & Profit Generator:** The **Insights** segment generates both the vast majority of total revenue (~80%+) and the overwhelming majority of operating profit. They are the same segment because published digital research can be sold to thousands of clients with virtually no extra production cost.

## Business Model Questions

### 1. What is the business model of the company?
Gartner operates a high-margin, asset-light content publishing model. It aggregates proprietary corporate data and practitioner research into digital reports and frameworks, then licenses this content to enterprise executives via recurring annual subscription agreements.

### 2. Is revenue recurring, one-time, or repeat but non-contractual?
* **Insights Segment:** Highly **recurring** via 12-month minimum subscription contracts (77% of contracts were multi-year as of December 31, 2025).
* **Conferences & Consulting Segments:** **Repeat, non-contractual/project-based**, depending on annual event attendance and individual consulting statements of work.

### 3. Where does pricing power come from?
Pricing power comes from Gartner's global brand recognition, its status as an independent enterprise IT benchmark, its proprietary database of executive decision metrics, and the strong network effects built around its industry-standard framework graphics (like the *Magic Quadrants*).

### 4. What would happen to revenue if their best-selling product was made obsolete by a competitor tomorrow?
If Gartner's core Insights subscription research platform was rendered obsolete, revenue would collapse by over 80%. Furthermore, its Conferences and Consulting business units would contract severely because Insights serves as the primary marketing funnel for those events and engagements.

### 5. Based on disclosures in the most recent annual report, are any customers responsible for more than 10% of revenue? If this is not disclosed in the filing, say "not disclosed" rather than guessing.
No single customer is responsible for 10% or more of Gartner's total revenue.

### 6. Based on the most recent annual report, what percentage of revenue comes from the top product or business segment? How has that percentage changed over the last three years?
The Insights business segment represents approximately 80% to 84% of total company revenue. This proportion has remained stable over the last three years (2023–2025), reflecting consistent top-line contribution across all three operating segments.

### 8. Which segment generates the most profit, and is that different from the one generating the most revenue?
The Insights segment generates the most profit, which is the same segment that generates the most revenue.

## Valuation Analysis

### 1. Current Price vs. Historical Average
Gartner's trailing P/E ratio is currently **~26.1x**, which sits **below its 5-year historical average of 33.14x**. While this appears "cheap" relative to its past valuation, the lower multiple follows a significant stock price drawdown caused by slowing subscription growth, goodwill impairment charges, and AI disruption fears.

### 2. Required Growth Assumptions
For today's market valuation to make sense over the next 3 to 5 years, Gartner's core Insights revenue must re-accelerate from its current ~2.5% rate back toward 6%–8% annually, operating margins must re-expand toward 20%, and its proprietary AI initiatives (*AskGartner*) must successfully defend its pricing power against free AI alternatives.

### 3. Events Causing Valuation Swings
* **High Valuation Drivers (2021–2023):** Surging corporate digital transformation budgets, record contract value (CV) growth, and aggressive share repurchases pushed Gartner's P/E multiple above 40x.
* **Low Valuation Drivers (2025–2026):** Cuts to forward Insights growth guidance (~2.5%), a **$150 million goodwill impairment charge** reported in late 2025, securities litigation alerts (*Bernstein Liebhard LLP* investigation), and Wall Street downgrades (e.g., Wells Fargo price target cut to $120; UBS cut to $164) led to valuation compression.

### 4. Valuation Assumptions & Realism Scores
* **Assumption 1: Generative AI tools (*AskGartner*) will preserve subscription pricing power.**
  * *Realism Score:* **4 / 10 (Low to Moderate)** — Enterprise clients have access to rapidly improving internal LLMs and free online research tools that commoditize standard research summaries.
* **Assumption 2: Insights contract growth rebounds above 5%.**
  * *Realism Score:* **5 / 10 (Moderate)** — Requires a major cyclical rebound in corporate enterprise IT spending and relaxed procurement budgets.
* **Assumption 3: Active share repurchases will continue supporting EPS growth.**
  * *Realism Score:* **8 / 10 (High)** — Gartner has demonstrated a long track record of buying back stock (e.g., executing hundreds of millions in buybacks under its ongoing multi-billion dollar program).

### 5. Essential Value Driver
Enterprise decision-makers must continue viewing Gartner's human expert network and vendor evaluations as irreplaceable, refusing to substitute them with in-house or free Generative AI tools.

### 6. Price Target Range & Analyst Disagreements

### 7. Area of Core Analyst Disagreement
Analysts are fundamentally split on **moat durability** (whether Generative AI enriches or destroys Gartner's research franchise) and **growth durability** (whether slowing contract growth is a temporary macro pause or permanent structural decay).

### 8. Consensus Overview

* **Consensus Price Target:** **$173.10**.

### 9. Expectations Check & Price Assessment
Gartner is currently trading around **$132 to $148**, which is **meaningfully below the consensus analyst target of $173.10**. The wide dispersion between analyst targets ($120 to $240) indicates that market expectations are unsettled. 

For the current price or a bullish recovery to be justified, an investor must believe that Gartner's Insights growth rate will stabilize above 5% and that its proprietary dataset cannot be replicated by modern AI applications.

## Risk Analysis

### 1. Three Biggest Specific Risks (3–5 Year Horizon)
1. **Generative AI Disruption & Moat Commoditization:** Rapid improvements in Large Language Models (LLMs) allow enterprise clients to synthesize software vendor comparisons, analyze peer reviews, and construct technology roadmaps without paying top-tier subscription fees.
2. **Contract Value (CV) Growth Deceleration & Client Churn:** Corporate cost discipline and vendor consolidation efforts could lead to enterprise client cancellations or tier downgrades at annual renewal time.
3. **Legal Overhang & Asset Impairments:** Ongoing securities fraud class action litigation and regulatory investigations (following recent $150 million goodwill impairment charges) could distract management and result in financial settlements.

### 2. Risk Classification
* **AI Commoditization:** *Industry-wide & Company-specific threat.* Plays out **gradually** as annual enterprise subscriptions come up for renewal.
* **Contract Value Slowdown:** *Macro & Industry-wide threat.* Plays out **gradually** along with enterprise budget cycles.
* **Litigation & Goodwill Impairments:** *Company-specific threat.* Plays out **suddenly** through legal disclosures or unexpected quarterly earnings adjustments.

### 3. The Bear Case Presentation
> "Gartner displays several characteristics of a value trap. While its statistical screens look attractive due to an asset-light business model, its core economic moat faces structural challenges. 
> 
> First, **Disruption**: Generative AI directly undermines Gartner's core value proposition—synthesizing vendor capabilities and writing technology summaries. When enterprise procurement teams can query AI tools for preliminary vendor comparisons, paying $50,000 to $100,000+ per year for human research becomes harder to justify.
> 
> Second, **Financial Deceleration**: Management recently cut full-year Insights revenue growth guidance to ~2.5%. Operating margins have dropped from ~21% to ~16%, total debt rose to $3.73 billion in 2025, and the company recorded a $150 million goodwill impairment charge.
> 
> Third, **Wall Street Realignment**: Major firms like Wells Fargo have downgraded the stock to Underweight with price targets as low as $120. Buying Gartner today relies heavily on the assumption that its *AskGartner* AI tool will preserve pricing power, which remains unproven against rapidly improving, lower-cost AI alternatives."

## Final Verdict

**BULL CASE SUMMARY:** Gartner surfaced on the Magic Formula screen as statistically cheap and high quality (Rank #24, Return on Capital of 242.81%, and Earnings Yield of 9.46%). It maintains an asset-light model with gross margins of ~68% and generates over $1.1 billion in annual free cash flow, backed by an established enterprise brand and active share repurchase programs.

**BEAR CASE SUMMARY:** Gartner faces a structural challenge from Generative AI tools that commoditize standard research summaries and vendor evaluations. Core Insights revenue growth has slowed toward ~2.5%, operating margins have compressed by over 500 basis points over three years, debt levels rose to $3.73 billion, and the company faces legal investigations alongside recent $150 million goodwill impairment charges. Wall Street analysts have issued downgrades with targets as low as $120.

**Verdict: Watch**

**Justification:** While Gartner's impressive historical return on capital (242.8%) and strong free cash flow generation ($1.18B in 2025) demonstrate the underlying strength of its asset-light model, the bear case presents valid concerns regarding core moat erosion. With Insights growth slowing to ~2.5% and operating margins contracting, it is unclear whether Gartner's proprietary AI initiatives (*AskGartner*) will offset the threat posed by free or low-cost Generative AI alternatives. Until contract value growth re-accelerates and the legal and financial impairment overhangs clear, initiating a new position is uncompelling. The stock is worth keeping on a watchlist to evaluate whether management can stabilize subscription growth and protect its pricing power.
