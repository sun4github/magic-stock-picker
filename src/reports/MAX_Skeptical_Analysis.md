## Basic Company Info Questions

### 1. What products or services does the company actually sell?
MediaAlpha operates an online ad technology platform (a digital marketplace). It sells **Consumer Referrals**—specifically high-intent insurance shoppers delivered via web clicks, phone calls, or direct lead form submissions—to insurance carriers and agents seeking new policyholders.

### 2. Who are its primary customers?
Its primary buyer customers (**Demand Partners**) are major Property & Casualty (P&C) auto and home insurance carriers (such as Progressive, GEICO, and Allstate), health insurers (primarily Medicare Advantage providers), and independent insurance agents. Its seller customers (**Supply Partners**) are websites, price comparison portals, or insurance carriers looking to monetize non-converting web traffic by redirecting shoppers to other insurers.

### 3. How does the company make money?
MediaAlpha earns a fee for every qualifying consumer action (click, phone call, or lead form) generated through its platform. Transactions occur either via an **Open Marketplace** (where MediaAlpha acts as an intermediary, setting terms and earning a spread) or a **Private Marketplace** (where buyers and sellers contract directly, and MediaAlpha collects a technology transaction fee).

### 4. Who are its two closest publicly traded competitors that compete most directly in the company’s largest revenue source, and what makes this company different from them? Explain why you chose those two.
The two closest direct competitors in digital insurance lead generation are **EverQuote, Inc. (EVER)** and **QuinStreet, Inc. (QNST)**.
* **Why chosen:** Both EverQuote and QuinStreet derive their primary revenue from matching high-intent insurance shoppers with P&C auto and health insurance carriers through performance-marketing platforms.
* **Key Differentiator:** Unlike competitors who rely heavily on buying consumer web traffic and arbitrage, MediaAlpha functions as a transparent, programmatic ad exchange with custom software integrations. It allows insurance carriers to act as both buyers and sellers (monetizing their own non-qualifying traffic) while providing granular bidding options across 35+ consumer attributes.

### 5. Write one sentence I could use to explain this business to a friend with no investing experience.
*"MediaAlpha runs an automated online ad marketplace that helps insurance companies buy web leads from active shoppers and sell off excess traffic they can't use."*

### 6. What is one common misunderstanding or oversimplification that could lead an investor to analyze this company incorrectly?
Investors often misclassify MediaAlpha as a standard software-as-a-service (SaaS) business or a stable digital advertising agency. In reality, it is a **highly cyclical middleman** whose revenue is tightly tied to auto insurance underwriting margins; when insurance companies face high claims losses, they abruptly cut off ad spending, causing MediaAlpha's transaction volumes to collapse.

### 7. Using the most recent annual report, what are the main business segments of the company, which generates the most revenue, and which generates the most profit? If those are different segments, explain why.
* **Main Segments / Verticals:** Property & Casualty (P&C) Insurance, Health Insurance (Medicare Advantage), and Life Insurance.
* **Most Revenue & Profit:** The **P&C Insurance vertical** generates both the vast majority of revenue (~93% of total revenue) and the largest share of profits (Transaction Value reached $1.9 billion out of $2.2 billion total in 2025). The health segment was scaled back substantially following regulatory settlement costs in under-65 health plans.

## Business Model Questions

### 1. What is the business model of the company?
MediaAlpha operates a programmatic, fee-based two-sided marketplace connecting insurance buyers (carriers/agents) with digital traffic sellers. It collects a fee per transaction without taking on inventory risk or underwriting liability.

### 2. Is revenue recurring, one-time, or repeat but non-contractual?
Revenue is **repeat but non-contractual**. While 99% of 2025 Transaction Value came from existing partner relationships, carriers do not sign fixed long-term spending commitments. Spending fluctuates dynamically based on daily bidding budgets and carrier profitability.

### 3. Where does pricing power come from?
Pricing power stems from **network density, data transparency, and deep API integrations**. As more carriers list their custom pricing rules on the platform, Supply Partners gain higher ad yields, which attracts more shopper traffic. Additionally, 106 Demand Partners maintaining direct pre-population integrations create technical lock-in.

### 4. What would happen to revenue if their best-selling product was made obsolete by a competitor tomorrow?
MediaAlpha’s primary product is its **P&C consumer click referrals** (accounting for 90.1% of Transaction Value). If a competitor rendered its click platform obsolete or bypassed it with cheaper direct carrier-to-publisher integrations, MediaAlpha’s revenue would decline by over 90%, crippling cash flows and threatening its ability to service its debt.

### 5. Based on disclosures in the most recent annual report, are any customers responsible for more than 10% of revenue?
**Not disclosed** in the general business narrative sections of the filing (though P&C ad spend as a collective category is dominated by four major national carriers: Progressive, Allstate, GEICO, and State Farm).

### 6. Based on the most recent annual report, what percentage of revenue comes from the top product or business segment? How has that percentage changed over the last three years?
The **P&C Insurance segment** generated approximately **93% of revenue** in 2025.
* **3-Year Trend:** P&C concentration increased from ~70-75% in 2023 to ~93% in 2025/2026. This shift occurred because P&C ad spend rebounded sharply while MediaAlpha intentionally scaled back its under-65 health insurance business following FTC regulatory action.

### 7. Which segment generates the most profit, and is that different from the one generating the most revenue?
The **P&C Insurance segment** generates the most revenue and the most profit; there is no divergence between the top revenue-generating and top profit-generating segment.

## Final Verdict

**BULL CASE:** MediaAlpha surfaced on the Magic Formula screen (#22 rank) due to an impressive statutory Return on Capital (292.67%) and an attractive Free Cash Flow yield (~9.6% to 12%). The cyclical recovery in P&C auto insurance advertising has driven revenue to $1.1 billion (+28.8% YoY) and generated $62.7M in FCF, supported by a scalable asset-light technology platform.

**BEAR CASE:** The business is heavily concentrated, with ~93% of revenue tied to P&C ad spending. MediaAlpha carries $163.5 million in debt against just $26.05 million in cash ($137.45M net debt). Governance and legal risks remain high following a $45 million FTC settlement for deceptive marketing and subsequent shareholder derivative investigations. At $13.32, the stock trades ~19% above the Wall Street consensus target ($11.17).

Verdict: Watch

**Justification:** While MediaAlpha’s cash generation and programmatic exchange technology are compelling bullish features, the stock is currently overextended relative to analyst targets ($13.32 market price vs. $11.17 consensus target). Combined with active fiduciary investigations, $163.5 million in debt, and extreme concentration in cyclical auto insurance ad budgets, initiating a new position at current levels carries unfavorable downside risk. A pullback toward or below $10.00 would offer a safer entry point.
