## Basic Company Info Questions

### 1. What products or services does the company actually sell?
Northern Oil and Gas, Inc. (**NOG**) sells crude oil, natural gas, and natural gas liquids (NGLs) extracted from North American shale basins.

### 2. Who are its primary customers?
Its primary customers are oil refineries, pipeline companies, energy marketers, and commodity traders who purchase physical crude oil and natural gas under spot-market or short-term contracts negotiated by third-party field operators.

### 3. How does the company make money?
NOG makes money by acquiring non-operating minority working interests (typically an average working interest of ~10% per well) in oil and natural gas properties. Third-party operators bear the operational burden of drilling and managing the wells. NOG pays its proportionate share of drilling, completion, and operating costs, and in return receives its proportionate share of revenues from the sale of oil and natural gas.

### 4. Who are its two closest publicly traded competitors that compete most directly in the company’s largest revenue source, and what makes this company different from them? Explain why you chose those two.
The two closest publicly traded competitors are **Civitas Resources, Inc. (CIVI)** and **Crescent Energy Company (CRGY)**. Both are independent North American exploration and production (E&P) companies of similar market capitalization ($2.3B to $3.8B) operating in premier shale regions like the Permian Basin. 

* **Key Difference:** Civitas and Crescent are traditional operators that physically run drilling rigs, manage field operations, and directly control capital expenditures and operating expenses. NOG, by contrast, is purely a non-operator. It owns zero drilling equipment, operates no wells, and relies entirely on third-party partners to execute field operations.

### 5. Write one sentence I could use to explain this business to a friend with no investing experience.
"Northern Oil and Gas acts like a silent financial partner in oil and gas wells, putting up a small share of the cash to let other companies drill, then taking a cut of the profits when the oil and gas are sold."

### 6. What is one common misunderstanding or oversimplification that could lead an investor to analyze this company incorrectly?
A common misunderstanding is viewing NOG as a traditional oil drilling company. Investors assuming NOG can cut operating expenses, alter drilling schedules, or improve field logistics during an industry downturn fail to realize that NOG has **zero operational control**. It cannot force an operator to drill or stop drilling, nor can it directly manage field-level operating costs.

### 7. Using the most recent annual report, what are the main business segments of the company, which generates the most revenue, and which generates the most profit? If those are different segments, explain why.
NOG operates and reports as a **single business segment** (oil and natural gas exploration and production). However, based on geographic basin disclosures in its 2025 Form 10-K, production and revenues are generated across four main basins:
* **Permian Basin:** 58.5 MBoe/d (42% of Q4 2025 daily production; 56% crude oil proved reserves)
* **Williston Basin:** 41.3 MBoe/d (30% of Q4 2025 daily production; 68% crude oil proved reserves)
* **Appalachian Basin:** 29.6 MBoe/d (21% of Q4 2025 daily production; 1% crude oil proved reserves)
* **Uinta Basin:** 10.7 MBoe/d (7% of Q4 2025 daily production; 90% crude oil proved reserves)

The **Permian Basin** generates the most revenue and profit due to its higher daily volume (42% of total production) and higher crude oil concentration (crude oil commands higher pricing and wider cash margins per unit than natural gas).

## Business Model Questions

### 1. What is the business model of the company?
NOG operates a non-operated capital allocation model (referred to internally as its "Ground Game"). It systematically acquires small, fragmented, non-operating working interests and mineral rights at discounts across top-tier US shale basins, partnering with over 100 experienced operators.

### 2. Is revenue recurring, one-time, or repeat but non-contractual?
Revenue is **repeat but non-contractual**. Well production declines naturally over time, and revenues fluctuate directly with commodity spot market price changes and well output.

### 3. Where does pricing power come from?
NOG has **zero pricing power**. Crude oil and natural gas are unorganized commodities sold at spot market benchmarks (such as NYMEX WTI for crude oil or Henry Hub for natural gas) minus regional transport differentials.

### 4. What would happen to revenue if their best-selling product was made obsolete by a competitor tomorrow?
Because crude oil accounts for the vast majority of NOG's top-line revenue, an immediate obsolescence of petroleum would wipe out revenues, trigger massive asset impairments, and render NOG unable to service its $2.4B debt load, leading to bankruptcy.

### 5. Based on disclosures in the most recent annual report, are any customers responsible for more than 10% of revenue?
**No single customer or operator represents more than 10% of total revenue.** The 2025 Form 10-K notes: *"Across these operators, no single operator represented more than 11% of our fourth quarter 2025 oil and natural gas sales."*

### 6. Based on the most recent annual report, what percentage of revenue comes from the top product or business segment? How has that percentage changed over the last three years?
Prior to 2020, 100% of NOG's production was concentrated in the oil-weighted Williston Basin. By Q4 2025, the Permian Basin grew to represent the single largest geographic contributor at **42% of total production**, followed by the Williston Basin (30%), Appalachian Basin (21%), and Uinta Basin (7%).

### 7. Which segment generates the most profit, and is that different from the one generating the most revenue?
The **Permian Basin** generates both the most revenue and the most profit, driven by high crude oil yields and strong net realized margins relative to natural gas-heavy assets like the Appalachian Basin.

## Risk Analysis

1. **Zero Operational & Cost Control:** Inability to prevent operating expense inflation or control drilling schedules set by third-party operators.
2. **Heavy Debt Load & Refinancing Risk:** $2.4B total debt and non-investment-grade credit ratings make NOG vulnerable during credit market tightenings or extended oil price slumps.
3. **Asymmetric Hedging Losses & Shareholder Dilution:** Derivatives cap upside during price rallies while frequent equity issuances (such as the 8M share issuance in early 2026) dilute long-term per-share compounding.

## Final Verdict

**BULL Case Summary:** Surfaced as a top statistical candidate in Joel Greenblatt's Magic Formula screen (#3 rank), NOG shows exceptional Return on Capital metrics (1,229%) and a high cheapness signal (12.9% earnings yield). Its non-operator model provides Geographic diversification across four premier US basins without exposure to direct rig equipment ownership, generating $252.8M in 2025 free cash flow to support an 8.2% dividend yield.

**BEAR Case Summary:** NOG operates with zero operational control over field costs or capital expenditure timing. It carries a heavy $2.40B debt load with non-investment-grade credit ratings, leaving it vulnerable during commodity slumps. Management habitually dilutes shareholders via equity offerings to fund its M&A addiction, while its derivative hedging program caps top-line revenue upside during crude rallies and introduces extreme GAAP earnings volatility.

Verdict: Watch

**Justification:** While NOG's statistical cheapness, high Magic Formula rank, and strong asset returns are attractive on paper, the underlying balance sheet leverage ($2.4B total debt) and lack of operational cost control introduce unacceptable risks in a cyclical energy market. Furthermore, serial equity dilution undermines per-share value growth. Investors should keep NOG on a watchlist and wait for management to demonstrate sustained deleveraging and a pause in dilutive acquisitions before initiating a position.
