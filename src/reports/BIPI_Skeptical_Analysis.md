### Basic Company Info Questions

1. **What products or services does the company actually sell?**  
   PT Astrindo Nusantara Infrastruktur Tbk (IDX: BIPI) provides energy infrastructure and logistical services in Indonesia. It owns and operates specialized midstream assets that move coal from mines to market, including overland conveyor belts, coal crushers, processing facilities, stockpiles, and deep-water coal loading ports.

2. **Who are its primary customers?**  
   Its primary customers are thermal coal mining companies operating in South and East Kalimantan, Indonesia, that require infrastructure to crush, process, and load mined coal onto shipping vessels.

3. **How does the company make money?**  
   BIPI earns revenue by charging fees per tonne of coal handled, crushed, and transported through its infrastructure (~90% of total revenue), as well as fixed rental and management fees for port services (~8.9%).

4. **Who are its two closest publicly traded competitors that compete most directly in the company’s largest revenue source, and what makes this company different from them? Explain why you chose those two.**  
   Its direct Indonesian publicly traded competitors in mining logistics and infrastructure services are **PT Delta Dunia Makmur Tbk (IDX: DOID)** and **PT Petrosea Tbk (IDX: PTRO)**. These two companies were chosen because they operate in the same geographic region (Indonesia) providing midstream mining support, contract mining, and hauling services. BIPI differs because it specializes primarily in fixed port and conveyor infrastructure assets rather than full-scale contract earthmoving and heavy equipment mining fleets.

5. **Write one sentence I could use to explain this business to a friend with no investing experience.**  
   "Astrindo Nusantara operates the specialized toll roads, conveyor belts, and deep-water ports that Indonesian coal miners pay to use so they can get their coal out of the mines and onto ships."

6. **What is one common misunderstanding or oversimplification that could lead an investor to analyze this company incorrectly?**  
   A major misunderstanding is assuming BIPI is a coal miner that profits directly from selling coal reserves. BIPI is a logistical infrastructure provider; its revenue depends on the *volume* of coal moved through its facilities. Furthermore, automated stock screeners (like the Magic Formula) can severely misread BIPI's quality because legacy accounting entries, M&A restructuring, and heavy debt distort automated metrics like Return on Capital.

7. **Using the most recent annual report, what are the main business segments of the company, which generates the most revenue, and which generates the most profit? If those are different segments, explain why.**  
   * **Main Segments:** Coal Mining Infrastructure & Logistical Services, Port Services, and Mining Services & Others.
   * **Most Revenue:** Coal Mining Infrastructure & Logistical Services generates ~90% of total revenue.
   * **Most Profit:** Coal Mining Infrastructure generates the largest absolute dollar profit due to its size, though Port Services historically earns higher profit margins per unit.

### Business Model Questions

1. **What is the business model of the company?**  
   BIPI operates a capital-intensive infrastructure model: it builds or acquires long-life logistical assets (conveyors, ports) and collects volume-based handling fees from coal producers using those assets.

2. **Is revenue recurring, one-time, or repeat but non-contractual?**  
   Revenue is repeat and contract-based, but variable—it fluctuates directly with customer coal production volumes and government-approved mining quotas (RKAB).

3. **Where does pricing power come from?**  
   Pricing power stems from owning captive, geographically strategically located infrastructure (like deep-water ports) near coal mines, where building an alternative competing conveyor or port is prohibitively expensive or legally restricted.

4. **What would happen to revenue if their best-selling product was made obsolete by a competitor tomorrow?**  
   Because BIPI's infrastructure assets are permanently fixed in specific geographic locations, a superior local competitor or an alternative transport route would cause BIPI's handling volumes and revenues to drop precipitously.

   Not disclosed in the summary filings provided.

6. **Based on the most recent annual report, what percentage of revenue comes from the top product or business segment? How has that percentage changed over the last three years?**  
   Coal Mining Infrastructure accounts for ~90% of revenue. Over the last three years, this percentage has remained consistently high (~90%–95%), showing that management's stated ambition to pivot into natural gas (LNG) and renewables has not yet meaningfully diversified revenue.

7. **Which segment generates the most profit, and is that different from the one generating the most revenue?**  
   Coal Mining Infrastructure generates both the most total revenue and the highest total dollar profit.

## Final Verdict

**BULL Case:** The stock surfaces on quantitative Magic Formula screens due to historical asset base calculations, possesses captive midstream port assets in Indonesia, and enjoys strategic ownership backing from major domestic conglomerates like the Bakrie Group (6.73% stake).

**BEAR Case:** BIPI is fundamentally decaying: 2025 revenue collapsed 58.2%, net margins turned negative (-2.8%), operating margins fell to 9.7%, and the company is choked by $747.7M in net debt (Net Debt-to-Equity of 120.66%). Servicing this debt while attempting an expensive business pivot will almost certainly require heavy equity dilution.

**Verdict: Avoid**

**Justification:** While BIPI may appear statistically cheap on mechanical value screens, its underlying fundamentals reveal an unacceptably high-risk profile. A 58% top-line collapse combined with a net loss and a net debt-to-equity ratio exceeding 120% leaves the company in a fragile financial position. Shareholders face severe risks of equity dilution from inevitable capital raises needed to fund its non-coal transition and service its high debt load. The bear case overwhelmingly nets out against the speculative bull case; investors should stay away.
