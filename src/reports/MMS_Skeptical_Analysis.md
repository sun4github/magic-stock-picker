### Skeptical Decomposer Analysis: Maximus, Inc. (NYSE: MMS)

### Basic Company Info Questions

1. **What products or services does the company actually sell?**
   Maximus provides tech-enabled business process outsourcing (BPO), clinical health evaluations, health insurance eligibility and enrollment administration, student loan support, and digital technology modernization services primarily to government agencies.

2. **Who are its primary customers?**
   Its primary customers are public sector government entities, consisting of U.S. federal agencies (56% of FY2025 revenue), U.S. state and local governments (32% of revenue), and international government bodies in the U.K., Canada, and the Middle East (11% of revenue).

3. **How does the company make money?**
   The company earns revenue through multi-year government contracts structured across four billing models:
   * **Performance-based / Transactional:** Billed per call, enrollment, or clinical assessment completed.
   * **Fixed-price:** Billed at a set rate for a defined scope of work.
   * **Cost-plus:** Reimbursed for direct/indirect costs plus a fixed profit margin or performance award fee.
   * **Time and materials:** Billed at fixed hourly labor rates.

4. **Who are its two closest publicly traded competitors that compete most directly in the company’s largest revenue source, and what makes this company different from them? Explain why you chose those two.**
   * **Competitors:** Leidos Holdings, Inc. (NYSE: LDOS) and Booz Allen Hamilton Holding Corp. (NYSE: BAH).
   * **Why chosen:** Maximus’s largest segment is U.S. Federal Services (56% of revenue). Leidos and Booz Allen are two of the largest publicly traded U.S. federal IT and BPO service contractors competing directly for agency modernization and administrative budgets.
   * **Differentiation:** While Leidos and Booz Allen focus heavily on defense, intelligence, and broad technical consulting, Maximus specializes specifically in human services, Medicaid/Medicare enrollment BPO, and clinical health evaluations (such as VA disability exams).

5. **Write one sentence I could use to explain this business to a friend with no investing experience.**
   "Maximus operates the behind-the-scenes call centers, health evaluations, and application processing systems that allow governments to run public benefit programs like Medicare, Medicaid, and Veterans Affairs disability claims."

6. **What is one common misunderstanding or oversimplification that could lead an investor to analyze this company incorrectly?**
   Investors often misclassify Maximus as a high-margin commercial software or tech company because of its "tech-enabled" branding. In reality, it is a labor-heavy government administrative contractor whose revenue growth and profit margins are bound by rigid public budgets, government procurement rules, and political policy changes.

7. **Using the most recent annual report, what are the main business segments of the company, which generates the most revenue, and which generates the most profit? If those are different segments, explain why.**
   * **Segments (FY2025):** U.S. Federal Services (56% of revenue), U.S. Services (32% of revenue), and Outside the U.S. (11% of revenue).
   * **Largest Revenue & Profit Generator:** The **U.S. Federal Services** segment generates both the most revenue (56%) and the highest operating profit, driven by high-volume, performance-based clinical contracts such as the Department of Veterans Affairs Medical Disability Examinations (MDE).

### Business Model Questions

1. **What is the business model of the company?**
   An asset-light, government-focused business process services (BPS) and IT integration model. It bids on multi-year public agency contracts to administer high-volume statutory programs at scale.

2. **Is revenue recurring, one-time, or repeat but non-contractual?**
   Revenue is contractual and repeat over multi-year contract terms (typically 3 to 10 years). However, contracts require periodic competitive rebids, and revenue includes temporary "surge" work (such as disaster response or emergency eligibility reviews) that eventually rolls off.

3. **Where does pricing power come from?**
   Pricing power stems from deep statutory domain expertise, strict regulatory compliance capabilities (e.g., FedRAMP cloud certifications), and the high operational risk/switching costs a government agency faces when swapping administrative infrastructure.

4. **What would happen to revenue if their best-selling product was made obsolete by a competitor tomorrow?**
   If a core federal contract (such as the CMS Contact Center Operations for 1-800-MEDICARE or the VA Medical Disability Examination contract) were lost to a competitor, revenue would instantly drop by several hundred million to over $1 billion (~20% of total company revenue from a single agency), causing sharp operating margin compression due to fixed corporate overhead.

5. **Based on disclosures in the most recent annual report, are any customers responsible for more than 10% of revenue? If this is not disclosed in the filing, say "not disclosed" rather than guessing.**
   Yes. According to the SEC Form 10-K, a single federal agency accounts for approximately **20% of total consolidated revenue**. Overall, the U.S. federal government accounts for 55% of total revenue.

6. **Based on the most recent annual report, what percentage of revenue comes from the top product or business segment? How has that percentage changed over the last three years?**
   The U.S. Federal Services segment generated **56% of total revenue** in FY2025. This segment's share of total revenue has increased over the past three years (up from prior years as international operations were divested and federal clinical contracts expanded).

7. **Which segment generates the most profit, and is that different from the one generating the most revenue?**
   The **U.S. Federal Services** segment generates both the highest revenue (56%) and the highest profit, benefiting from performance-based clinical assessment contracts that yield higher margins than standard BPO or international operations.

### Risk Analysis

1. **Three Biggest Specific Risks Over the Next 3 to 5 Years:**

   * **Risk 1: High Customer & Contract Concentration (Company-Specific, Sudden Impact)**
     * *Explanation:* With 55% of revenue from federal agencies and ~60% from its top 10 contracts (and ~20% from a single agency), losing a major contract rebid (e.g., CMS 1-800-MEDICARE or VA MDE) would immediately cut revenue and earnings.

   * **Risk 2: In-Sourcing & Direct Agency AI Automation (Industry-Wide, Gradual Impact)**
     * *Explanation:* As AI and automated workflow platforms mature, state and federal agencies may build internal administrative tools rather than outsourcing contact center BPO to third-party vendors.

   * **Risk 3: Federal Spending Priorities & Privatization Restrictions (Macro / Regulatory, Gradual to Sudden Impact)**
     * *Explanation:* Legislative budget cuts or statutory restrictions preventing private entities from managing public benefit programs could shrink Maximus’s total addressable market (TAM).

2. **Bear Case Presentation to Investment Committee:**
   * *Revenue Fragility:* Despite adjusted EPS beats, consolidated revenues have contracted due to the expiration of disaster support contracts. This highlights that recent growth was inflated by temporary emergency work rather than permanent organic demand.
   * *Rebid & Margin Squeeze:* As contracts come up for rebid, competitors (Leidos, Booz Allen, Conduent) can undercut Maximus on price, forcing margin concessions on fixed-price arrangements.
   * *Automation Disintermediation:* If generative AI reduces the labor required to run contact centers and process paperwork, government clients will demand lower contract values, shrinking Maximus's overall billable revenue.

## Final Verdict

**BULL CASE:** The Magic Formula screen highlights Maximus as statistically cheap and high-quality, ranking #20 with an impressive 93.14% Return on Capital and a 13.73% Earnings Yield. The company generates robust free cash flow ($366M+ in FY25), has deleveraged its balance sheet to 1.75x Net Debt/EBITDA, and trades at single-digit earnings multiples (~8.2x P/E) well below its historical average of 19.7x and analyst target consensus of $110.

**BEAR CASE:** Revenues are experiencing near-term top-line contraction as post-pandemic surge work and disaster support contracts roll off. The business suffers from extreme customer concentration, with ~60% of revenues coming from just 10 contracts and 20% tied to a single federal agency. Furthermore, government clients may increasingly leverage internal AI tools, threatening long-term BPO outsourcing demand.

Verdict: Watch

**Justification:** While Maximus’s statistical cheapness, high Return on Capital, and strong cash flow generation make it an intriguing value candidate, the bear case highlights real structural risks around top-line contraction and customer concentration. With disaster surge revenues unwinding and key federal contract rebids ahead, the stock is not compelling enough to initiate an immediate position until top-line revenue stabilizes and rebid visibility improves. Therefore, it belongs on a watchlist to monitor for a stable organic growth catalyst.
