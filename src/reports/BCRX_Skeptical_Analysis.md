### Basic Company Info Questions

**1. What products or services does the company actually sell?**  
BioCryst Pharmaceuticals primary product is **ORLADEYO** (berotralstat), an oral, once-daily prescription capsule designed to prevent swelling attacks in patients with Hereditary Angioedema (HAE). It also sells an oral pellet formulation of ORLADEYO for young children (ages 2 to under 12) and **RAPIVAB** (peramivir injection), an intravenous treatment for acute influenza. 

**2. Who are its primary customers?**  
Its primary customers are specialty pharmacy providers and drug distributors (such as EMPOWER Patient Services in the United States) that deliver high-cost rare disease treatments directly to HAE patients, as well as hospitals and government agencies purchasing influenza antivirals.

**3. How does the company make money?**  
BioCryst generates money through three channels:
* Direct product sales of ORLADEYO to specialty pharmacies in the U.S.
* Royalty payments and milestone fees from global licensing partners (e.g., Torii Pharmaceutical in Japan, Pint Pharma in Latin America, and Neopharmed Gentili in Europe).
* Contract sales of RAPIVAB to government stockpiles and healthcare institutions.

**4. Who are its two closest publicly traded competitors that compete most directly in the company’s largest revenue source, and what makes this company different from them? Explain why you chose those two.**  
* **Pharvaris N.V. (NASDAQ: PHVS):** Pharvaris is developing deucrictibant, an investigational oral therapy for HAE. Pharvaris was chosen because it directly threatens BioCryst's main competitive advantage: being the only targeted daily oral preventative pill on the market.
* **Intellia Therapeutics, Inc. (NASDAQ: NTLA):** Intellia is developing NTLA-2002, a CRISPR-based gene-editing candidate designed to provide a potential single-dose, permanent reduction in HAE swelling attacks. Intellia was chosen because it represents a disruptive technological threat that could eliminate the need for daily preventative therapy altogether.
* **Key Difference:** BioCryst currently offers an *approved, daily oral pill* already on the market, whereas Pharvaris and Intellia are clinical-stage companies whose competing treatments are still undergoing clinical trials.

**5. Write one sentence I could use to explain this business to a friend with no investing experience.**  
BioCryst sells a daily preventative pill that stops life-threatening swelling attacks in people born with a rare genetic disorder called Hereditary Angioedema.

**6. What is one common misunderstanding or oversimplification that could lead an investor to analyze this company incorrectly?**  
An investor might look at BioCryst's massive 2025 GAAP net income ($263.8 million) and low Price-to-Earnings ratio (~6.2x) and conclude the company is suddenly hyper-profitable. In reality, 2025 net income was temporarily inflated by a one-time **$250 million cash sale** of its European ORLADEYO commercial rights to Neopharmed Gentili. Looking at unadjusted 2025 numbers creates a false picture of underlying recurring profitability, as evidenced by a return to quarterly net losses in early 2026.

**7. Using the most recent annual report, what are the main business segments of the company, which generates the most revenue, and which generates the most profit? If those are different segments, explain why.**  
BioCryst operates primarily as a single integrated business segment focused on rare disease therapies. Within its product lines:
* **ORLADEYO (HAE Franchise):** Generates over 90% of recurring product sales and virtually all operating profit.
* **RAPIVAB (Influenza):** Generates minor residual revenue. The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) notified BioCryst in May 2025 that it would not renew optional ordering periods on its procurement contract, diminishing this segment's future contribution.

### Business Model Questions

**1. What is the business model of the company?**  
BioCryst operates a specialty biopharmaceutical business model. It relies heavily on marketing a single high-margin commercial therapy (ORLADEYO) in major markets like the U.S., while outsourcing international distribution to local partners to keep overhead low. Following a major strategic shift in June 2026, the company permanently closed its internal Birmingham, Alabama research facility to pivot away from early-stage internal drug discovery toward acquiring or in-licensing late-stage external clinical assets.

**2. Is revenue recurring, one-time, or repeat but non-contractual?**  
Revenue is **repeat and chronic**: HAE is a lifelong genetic disorder, so patients who tolerate ORLADEYO take the drug daily indefinitely, creating stable repeat prescription refills.

**3. Where does pricing power come from?**  
Pricing power stems from **orphan drug exclusivity** (regulatory protection granted to treatments for rare conditions) and the significant patient convenience of an oral pill compared to frequent, painful intravenous or subcutaneous injections.

**4. What would happen to revenue if their best-selling product was made obsolete by a competitor tomorrow?**  
Because ORLADEYO accounts for virtually all recurring product revenue, product obsolescence would wipe out over 90% of BioCryst’s cash inflows, forcing the company to rely on cash reserves and pre-commercial pipeline assets.

**5. Based on disclosures in the most recent annual report, are any customers responsible for more than 10% of revenue?**  
**Yes.** In the United States, sales flow through a highly concentrated network of specialty pharmacy distributors. A small number of specialized specialty pharmacy partners account for the vast majority of U.S. ORLADEYO revenue.

**6. Based on the most recent annual report, what percentage of revenue comes from the top product or business segment? How has that percentage changed over the last three years?**  
ORLADEYO accounted for **over 90%** of total product revenue in 2025. This percentage has steadily increased over the last three years (from ~80% in 2023) as ORLADEYO patient uptake grew globally while legacy flu drug (RAPIVAB) revenues contracted.

**7. Which segment generates the most profit, and is that different from the one generating the most revenue?**  
The **ORLADEYO HAE Franchise** generates both the most revenue and the most profit; there is no divergence between the two.

### Risk Analysis

**1. What are the three biggest specific risks over the next three to five years?**

* **Risk 1: Competitive Obsolescence of ORLADEYO (Industry-Wide | Gradual to Sudden)**  
  * *Context:* Rival biotech Pharvaris is advancing an oral HAE drug (deucrictibant), while Intellia is advancing a single-dose CRISPR gene-editing treatment. If a rival oral drug demonstrates superior attack reduction or if gene editing proves safe, ORLADEYO’s market share could collapse rapidly.
* **Risk 2: Execution Failure of the New Business Model Pivot (Company-Specific | Gradual)**  
  * *Context:* In June 2026, BioCryst announced the permanent wind-down of its internal drug discovery programs and the closure of its Birmingham research facility. Relying 100% on external deal-making/in-licensing removes native research capabilities. Sourcing quality assets externally is capital-intensive and carries high failure rates.
* **Risk 3: Loss of Exclusivity and Patent Cliff Post-2030 (Company-Specific | Sudden)**  
  * *Context:* Primary patents protecting ORLADEYO begin expiring after 2030. Without a commercially successful secondary pipeline asset (such as navenibart or BCX17725) generating meaningful revenue before 2030, BioCryst faces a severe revenue cliff.
