# Skeptical Decomposer Analysis: ADT Inc. (NYSE: ADT)

---

## Basic Company Info Questions

### 1. What products or services does the company actually sell?
ADT sells residential and small business security systems, smart home automation technology, and professional monitoring services. Its offerings include burglary and life-safety alarms, smart cameras, video surveillance, environmental sensors (smoke, fire, carbon monoxide, water leak detection), personal emergency response systems, and software access via its proprietary **ADT+** application.

### 2. Who are its primary customers?
Its primary customers are single-family homeowners, renters, and small business owners across the United States.

### 3. How does the company make money?
ADT generates revenue in two main ways:
* **Upfront Installation & Equipment Sales:** Charging customers upfront (or through monthly installment payment plans) for hardware installation and setup.
* **Recurring Monthly Revenue (RMR):** Charging subscription fees under multi-year contracts (typically 2, 3, or 5 years) for 24/7 professional alarm monitoring, smart home software integration, and system maintenance.

### 4. Who are its two closest publicly traded competitors that compete most directly in the company’s largest revenue source, and what makes this company different from them?
* **Alarm.com Holdings, Inc. (ALRM):** Provides cloud platform software powering interactive security and smart home systems for independent security dealers. *Difference:* Alarm.com is a software platform and technology provider that sells through a network of independent service providers, whereas ADT is a direct-to-consumer vertically integrated provider that sells, installs, and monitors its own hardware and service plans directly.
* **Resideo Technologies, Inc. (REZI):** Manufactures and distributes home security products, thermostats, and comfort solutions (spun off from Honeywell). *Difference:* Resideo focuses heavily on hardware manufacturing and wholesale distribution, whereas ADT’s core profit engine is high-margin, recurring 24/7 professional monitoring subscriptions.

### 5. Plain-Language Explanation
> "ADT installs security alarms and smart home gadgets in houses and small businesses, then charges monthly fees to monitor those alarms around the clock and alert emergency services if something goes wrong."

### 6. Common Misunderstanding / Oversimplification
A major misunderstanding is treating ADT as purely a hardware or "gadget" company. Investors often assume ADT competes head-to-head on device pricing against low-cost do-it-yourself (DIY) camera makers. In reality, ADT is a **subscription service and customer acquisition machine**—its financial health depends on how much cash it spends up front to sign up a subscriber versus how long that subscriber stays under multi-year contract before cancelling (*attrition rate*).

### 7. Main Business Segments & Profitability
According to Item 1 and Item 3 of ADT’s SEC Form 10-K, ADT evaluates performance and allocates resources on a consolidated basis and reports as a **single operating and reportable segment** (focused on residential and small business security and smart home solutions). This follows its divestitures of its Commercial business, ADT Solar, and its multifamily business. Because ADT reports as a single segment, one segment generates all revenue and operating profit.

## Business Model Questions

### 1. What is the business model of the company?
ADT operates a **subscriber-based, capital-intensive service model**. The company spends significant upfront cash (*Customer Acquisition Cost* or CAC) to subsidize labor, sales commissions, and equipment for new installations. It recovers this initial capital outflow over time by collecting recurring monthly subscription fees for professional monitoring and interactive software services over multi-year contracts.

### 2. Revenue Characteristics
Revenue is **primarily recurring and contractual** via 2- to 5-year monitoring agreements with automatic renewal provisions, supplemented by **one-time upfront installation and equipment fees**.

### 3. Source of Pricing Power
ADT’s pricing power stems from its **150+ year legacy brand recognition**, deep professional monitoring infrastructure (6 redundant Underwriters Laboratories-listed monitoring centers), integration with home insurance discounts, and high switching costs created by long-term contracts and professionally installed physical hardware.

### 4. Impact of Best-Selling Product Obsoletion
If ADT’s core interactive monitoring or smart camera platform were rendered obsolete overnight, its upfront hardware installation revenue would vanish, and customer attrition (*cancellations*) would spike dramatically. However, revenue would not drop to zero immediately due to legally binding multi-year contracts (which contain early cancellation fee penalties), providing a short-term cash buffer while requiring heavy capital expenditure to rebuild technology offerings.

### 5. Customer Concentration Disclosure
Based on disclosures in the Form 10-K, **no single customer accounts for more than 10% of revenue**, as ADT's customer base is widely dispersed across approximately 6.1 million residential and small business subscribers.

### 6. Segment Revenue Concentration & 3-Year Trend
Because ADT reports as **a single reportable segment**, 100% of revenue originates from this core segment. Over the last three years, ADT streamlined its business by exiting ADT Solar and divesting its Commercial and multifamily business units to focus strictly on its core residential and small business segment.

### Financial Performance Breakdown
* **Revenue Trend:** **Improving.** Upward trajectory fueled by price increases and steady core subscription demand, despite divesting non-core assets.
* **Margin Trend:** **Declining to Stable.** Gross margins faced modest pressure (dropping from 51.1% to 49.25%) due to labor cost inflation and installation subsidies, though operating margins stabilized near 26%.
* **Free Cash Flow:** **Improving.** FCF expanded meaningfully from $851M in 2023 to $1.31B in 2025, benefiting from disciplined capital expenditures and operational efficiency initiatives (like remote technical support).
* **Debt Levels:** **Stable / High.** Total debt remains heavy at over $7.7B (Net Debt ~$7.61B), though leverage ratios improved slightly to ~2.66x Net Debt/EBITDA.

### Single Most Important Financial Risk
**Debt Service Burden in a High-Rate Environment.** While free cash flow looks healthy, ADT’s total debt (~$7.71B) is greater than its entire stock market capitalization (~$6.72B). Net interest coverage sits at a tight **2.0x–3.2x**, leaving the company exposed to refinancing risks and absorbing cash that could otherwise fund growth or cushion against rising subscriber attrition.

## Valuation Analysis

### 1. Historical Relative Valuation
ADT’s trailing P/E of **11.2x** is cheap relative to its 5-year historical average P/E of **12.83x**, and trades at an enterprise value multiple of ~5.0x EV/EBITDA.

### 2. What Must Prove True for Today's Price to Make Sense?
For today's stock price (~$6.15 - $6.60 range) to generate solid investor returns over the next 3–5 years:
1. Subscriber attrition must remain steady or decline below 12-13%.
2. ADT must successfully retain customers on its new **ADT+ / Google Nest** platform without heavily increasing subsidization costs.
3. Free cash flow must remain above $1.0B annually to pay down maturing debt and maintain the ~2.8% dividend yield.

### 3. Valuation Drivers & Historical Patterns
* **High Valuation Periods (2020–2022):** Driven by strategic announcements, including Google’s initial equity investment/commercial partnership ($450M commitment) and State Farm's $300M development partnership announcement in 2022.
* **Low Valuation / Depressed Periods (2024–2026):** Caused by the expiration of the State Farm Opportunity Fund in late 2025 (and repayment of unspent funds), flat full-year 2026 guidance, and most notably in May 2026, when private equity firm **Apollo Global Management executed a complete exit via a 102 million-share secondary offering**, flooding the market with share supply.

### 6. Where Analysts Disagree
Analysts are divided on:

* **Margin Pressure & Labor Inflation:** Rising installer/field technician labor wages threaten profit margins.
* **Partnership Longevity:** The roll-off of State Farm partnership funds creates distribution uncertainty.

### 7. Expectations Check
Trading at ~11.2x earnings and a ~19.5% FCF yield, the market is **pricing in stagnant to ultra-low growth (0-2%)** and persistent structural debt risks. The market is betting that ADT is a low-growth legacy cash generator rather than a growth enterprise.

## Risk Analysis

### 1. Three Biggest Specific Risks (3–5 Year Horizon)

1. **DIY Industry Disruption & High Customer Attrition (Industry-Wide / Gradual)**
   * *Mechanism:* Consumers increasingly favor no-contract, low-cost self-installed systems (Ring, SimpliSafe, Google Nest DIY). If residential attrition rises, ADT's high upfront cost to acquire subscribers (*payback period ~2 years*) turns non-economical, destroying long-term cash flows.
2. **Heavy Debt Overhang & Refinancing Sensitivity (Company-Specific / Sudden to Gradual)**
   * *Mechanism:* With ~$7.71B in total debt and net interest coverage hovering around ~2.0x–3.2x, elevated interest rates increase interest expense during debt refinancing rounds, eating directly into net profit and dividend coverage.
3. **Distribution & Partnership Deterioration (Company-Specific / Sudden)**
   * *Mechanism:* Following the expiration of the State Farm Opportunity Fund in late 2025 and Apollo’s complete equity exit in May 2026, ADT faces heightened pressure to independently drive customer acquisition without major partner subsidies.

### 2. Bear Case Presentation to an Investment Committee
> **"Why We Should Reject ADT Today:"**
> 
> * **Weak Balance Sheet Flexibility:** ADT carries $7.7B in total debt—exceeding its market cap. Reported GAAP net profit margins have compressed sharply toward ~0.1% to 1.1%, leaving minimal margin for error. Net interest coverage is narrow at ~2.0x net interest cover, and dividend payments are poorly covered by GAAP net income (requiring reliance on adjusted non-GAAP FCF).
> * **Growth Stagnation:** Management’s guidance for 2026 top-line growth is essentially flat. The company relies heavily on price hikes on existing subscribers rather than net organic subscriber additions.
> * **Brand & Product Clutter:** ADT continues to struggle in DIY branding, launching overlapping offerings like "ADT Blu" on top of "Blue by ADT" and "ADT Self Setup," confusing potential self-install customers while facing intense wage inflation for its professional installation field workforce.
> * **Loss of Key Backers:** Private equity owner Apollo fully exited its 102M share stake in May 2026, dumping significant share volume onto the public market and signaling that institutional sponsors see limited remaining LBO-style upside.

## Final Verdict

**BULL CASE:** The Magic Formula screen highlights ADT as a highly compelling statistical value (#11 rank), pairing a cheap **10.29% Earnings Yield** with an extraordinarily high **757.99% Return on Capital** (driven by ADT's accounting structure of operating a lean tangible asset base backed by recurring contract cash flows). The company generates over **$1.3B in annual Free Cash Flow** (~19.5% FCF yield), maintains strong 26% operating margins, and trades at a modest 11.2x P/E—well below historical averages and Wall Street's $8.95 consensus target.

**BEAR CASE:** ADT operates under a severe debt burden ($7.71B total debt vs. $6.72B market cap) with constrained net interest coverage (~2.0x), flat 2026 revenue guidance, compressed net profit margins, rising field labor costs, and ongoing market disruption from low-cost DIY competitors. Furthermore, the complete exit of long-time owner Apollo Global Management via a 102M share offering removes an institutional floor while the expiration of State Farm partnership funds adds channel friction.

Verdict: Watch

**Justification:** ADT presents a classic value-trap paradox: its core monitoring contracts generate massive, reliable free cash flow ($1.3B+) that easily satisfies Greenblatt's Magic Formula quality and cheapness thresholds, yet its massive debt load and structural labor costs severely limit equity upside. While the stock is undeniably cheap on an FCF yield basis (~19.5%) and offers a 2.8% dividend yield, flat 2026 growth guidance and narrow interest coverage mean new investors should wait on the sidelines. A "Watch" rating is warranted until management proves it can consistently reduce net debt below 2.0x EBITDA and demonstrate clean subscriber additions on its new ADT+/AI video platform following Apollo's secondary exit.
