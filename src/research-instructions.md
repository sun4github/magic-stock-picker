# Goal
Define the comprehensive set of instructions that will guide a decomposer agent to structure research tasks in the most critical and efficient way possible for a given stock ticker.

# Must follow instructions
1. Questions should always challenge the established facts and not conform it. 
2. Example questions: What risks am I overlooking ? Wjat would a bear argue against this ? Does this make sense ?

# Key metrics for  Business Types
1.**Business type**: Software / SaaS 
**Key metrics**: Revenue growth, gross margin, free
cash flow, retention rate
2.**Business type**: Retail / Consumer 
**Key metrics**: Same-store sales, inventory turns,
operating margin, membership metrics
3.**Business type**: Semiconductor / Cyclical 
**Key metrics**: Gross margin, inventory levels, CapEx
spending, order backlog
4.**Business type**: Financial (Banks, Insurance) 
**Key metrics**: ROE, credit quality, net interest margin
(banks), loss ratios (insurance)
5.**Business type**: Asset-Light Compounder 
**Key metrics**: Margins, free cash flow, return on
invested capital, network effects

# Assumptions
1. Assume your audience/readers are smart beginner investors with no prior knowledge of the company being analyzed.
2. Assume that you are skeptical about the company and you are trying to find reasons not to invest in it. 
3. Always use publicly available information from reliable sources.
4. Always cite your sources.
5. Always use clear, concise, and easy-to-understand language.
6. Do not use technical jargon or complex terminology unless it is absolutely necessary. When it is, provide a clear explanation of the term.
7.**CRITICAL** Do not invent facts or information. It is better to say "Information not available" than to invent facts.


# Research Instructions

## Basic Company Info Questions
1. What products or services does the company actually sell?
2. Who are its primary customers?
3. How does the company make money?
4. Who are its two closest publicly traded competitors that compete most directly in the company’s largest revenue source, and what makes this company different from them? Explain why you chose those two. 
5. Write one sentence I could use to explain this business to a friend with no investing experience.
6. What is one common misunderstanding or oversimplification that could lead an investor to analyze this company incorrectly?
7. Using the most recent annual report, what are the main business segments of the company, which generates the most revenue, and which generates the most profit? If those are different segments, explain why.

## Business Model Questions
1. What is the business model of the company? 
2. Is revenue recurring, one-time, or repeat but non-contractual? 
3. Where does pricing power come from? 
4. What would happen to revenue if their best-selling product was made obsolete by a competitor tomorrow? 
5. Based on disclosures in the most recent annual report, are any customers responsible for more than 10% of revenue? If this is not disclosed in the filing, say "not disclosed" rather than guessing.
6. Based on the most recent annual report, what percentage of revenue comes from the top product or business segment? How has that percentage changed over the last three years? 
8. Which segment generates the most profit, and is that different from the one generating the most revenue?

## Key numbers
Using the Income Statement, Cash Flow Statement,
and Balance Sheet from the most recent annual report answer the following questions. If they are not readily available search stockanalysis.com or company investor relations page.
1. Pull out the following in a simple table format for last three years : revenue growth, gross margin, operating margin, free cash flow, and total debt. Note any trends. 
Note: free cash flow equals operating cash flow minus capital expenditures and is found on the Cash Flow Statement, not the Income Statement. Always state your sources.
2. Looking at revenue trend, margin trend, free cash flow, and debt levels separately, is each improving,declining, or stable? 
3. What is the single most important financial risk across all four areas?

## Comparitive analysis
Use stockanalysis.com or Yahoo finance or other publicly available datasources to gather the current trailing twelve month P/E ratio and the key financial metrics for the company and its two closest publicly traded competitors and answer the following questions for the company we are interested in and its two closest competitors.
1. Based on these metrics, explain whether the differences in revenue growth, gross margin, operating margin, and
valuation reflect a structural business model advantage or a performance gap. 
2. Which company appears strongest on each metric and why?

## Valuation analysis
Use stockanalysis.com or other publicly available datasources to compare company's current trailing twelve month P/E ratio to its own five-year historical average and answer the following questions for the company we are interested in.
1. Does the current price look expensive, fair, or cheap relative to its own history? 
2. In plain language, what would need to be true about this company's growth over the next three to five years for today's price to make sense?
3. What specific events or conditions caused periods of high valuation and low valuation for the company over the last five years? Based on those patterns, what conditions today look most similar to a previous period of high valuation? Cite any specific news sources for events within last year.
4. List all assumptions and give a realism score for them.
5. What assumptions must prove true from here to justify today's price? 
6. What is the range of the highest and lowest price targets? What key assumptions explain that range?
7. Where are analysts actually disagreeing: moat durability, growth rate, margin stability, capital intensity,
regulation, cycle timing, or other factors?
8. What is the analyst consensus on price targets, and number of analysts covering teh security ? 
9. Based on all the above data answer this:
State whether the security is trading above or below consensus. If dispersion between targets is wide, interpret it as expectations not being settled. If the security is meaningfully above or below consensus, identify what assumptions about growth durability, margin structure, and the duration of growth would have to prove true for the current market price to be justified under a reasonable bullish interpretation.
**Guidance**: You are not asking whether a stock is cheap or
expensive. You are instead asking what the current price is betting on.
**Caution 1**: Historical P/E comparisons are less useful for cyclical businesses such as semiconductors, automakers, airlines, and commodity companies. For these, focus more
heavily on the Expectations Check below and on industry conditions.
**Caution 2**: For companies trading at extremely high valuation multiples where current earnings are not representative of the long-term opportunity, historical valuation comparisons may provide little insight. In these cases, place greater emphasis on the Expectations Check and focus on what future assumptions the market is pricing in.

## Risk analysis
Given all the facts gathered above, answer the following questions: 
1. what are the three biggest specific risks over the next three to five years? 
2. For each risk indicate whether it is company-specific, industry-wide, or macro, and whether it would likely play out gradually or suddenly. Avoid generic risks unless you explain exactly how they apply to this specific company.
3. Assume you are presenting the bear case to an investment committee determined to avoid mistakes. What are the strongest reasons to reject this company as an investment today? Focus on valuation, competition, management execution, industry disruption, and ways my thesis could be wrong. Challenge assumptions rather than repeating them.
